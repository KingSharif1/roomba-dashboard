1. The Roomba that kept hitting the same wall

I got a Roomba 694 off Facebook Marketplace. Then I watched it work.

It bumped into a wall, went around, came back, and bumped the same wall again. It wasn't smart at all.

The engineer in me knew a Raspberry Pi and an Arduino could make it way better. I used to love watching Pi and Arduino DIY videos. This time, with AI, I didn't have to watch five to ten videos just to figure out if I had the right model. I ordered the wire and got started.

2. What I added

Raspberry Pi 4

The brain. Ubuntu, ROS 2 Jazzy, mapping and voice.

RPLIDAR C1

Spins 360° to map the apartment.

Pi Camera

Looks forward from just in front of the Pi.

Arduino Mega

Reflexes. Talks to the Roomba over serial.

16x2 LCD

Status at a glance: battery, lidar, mode.

MPU6050

Heading and tilt, so she knows which way she turned.

Speaker + mic

A voice. Wake word: "Stella".

Power chain

Fuse → cutoff → switch → 5 A buck. Clean 5.15 V from the Roomba battery.

3. How it fits

The main board and battery live inside the Roomba. Lidar sits on four 35 mm posts so its beam clears everything (≈ 65 mm above the plate).

4. How it's wired

Mini-DIN colors are from my cable, traced with a meter. VPWR is raw battery (~15 V): never on the Arduino or Pi. Green (BRC) is unused for now. No buzzer: the Roomba plays beeps itself (OI Song/Play).

5. Timeline

  1. 2026-08-23Started the web dashboard repo. Mock data only, but it was the first line of Stella.
  2. Late August · days 1–3 blog · part 1Found the Roomba on Facebook Marketplace for $40. Most of the expensive parts (Pi 4, Mega, camera, MPU6050) came from a robotics kit my friend Eback gave me. Installed Ubuntu Server 24.04 and ROS 2 Jazzy on the Pi, built the Roomba and lidar drivers.
  3. Late August · day 2Wrong cable. I ordered an 8-pin MD8; the Roomba's port is a 7-pin mini-DIN. It kind of fits, which made it worse. Two days of silence on the serial lines.
  4. Late August · day 3RPLIDAR C1 arrived and spun up over USB on the first try.
  5. SeptemberThe right cable (Adafruit #2438) arrived, and the Roomba still wouldn't talk. The original board is the Wi-Fi model; its firmware blocked the serial port. Signal pins sat at 0 V.
  6. 2026-09-23Ordered a used 600-series main board on eBay (~$22).
  7. 2026-09-29 blog · part 2Swapped the board, traced every wire with a meter, and got two-way serial. By night the Roomba was driving from my code, streaming sensors, and the lidar was publishing scans in ROS 2. Manual drive went live on the dashboard.
  8. 2026-10-11Locked out of the Pi after a 10-day break; reset it headless and switched to key login. Named her Stella and gave her a logo.
  9. 2026-10-11The Pi died. Weeks on laptop USB and a weak power bank: under-voltage, throttling at 85 °C, then no boot. The Mega and lidar survived.
  10. 2026-10-11Redesigned power around the Roomba battery: 3 A fuse, XH-M609 cutoff, rocker, DROK 5 A buck at 5.15 V. Planned the 10 in deck and ordered the last parts.
  11. 2026-10-12Finished the diagnosis, ordered a replacement Vilros Pi 4 kit, wrote the safety and rebuild plan.
  12. NextRebuild on the new Pi → mount the lidar → first map → voice.

7. What it took

I didn't think it would be easy, but I didn't expect how much patience it would take. The worst part is getting a part and it's the wrong one, or it's a whole different issue, and then waiting again.

I learned a lot: hosting servers on a Pi, the Arduino, every part of the robot and how it all connects to the motherboard, in a smart but messy way. I learned the hard way why the newer 694 board won't work: on the Wi-Fi model, the firmware locks you out of the serial port (the Open Interface). An older 600-series board fixed it.

After a month or two, I believe I have all the parts. I ended up spending money I didn't expect, about $240 on the latest round.

Rules I learned
Get the right part the first time.
Ground first, always.
Never trust wire colors.
Never power a Pi from a power bank.
