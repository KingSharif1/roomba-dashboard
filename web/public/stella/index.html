1. The Roomba that kept hitting the same wall

I got a Roomba 694 off Facebook Marketplace. Then I watched it work.

It bumped into a wall, went around, came back, and bumped the same wall again. It wasn't smart at all.

The engineer in me knew a Raspberry Pi and an Arduino could make it way better. I used to love watching Pi and Arduino DIY videos. This time, with AI, I didn't have to watch five to ten videos just to figure out if I had the right model. I ordered the wire and got started.

Base
Roomba 694, $40
Li-ion 14.4 V (16.8 V full)
iRobot Open Interface
serial · 115200 baud

2. What I added

Raspberry Pi 4

The brain. Ubuntu, ROS 2 Jazzy, mapping and voice.

RPLIDAR C1

Spins 360° to map the apartment.

Pi Camera

Looks forward from just in front of the Pi.

CP2102 adapters

Two tiny USB-serial boards. One talks to the Roomba, one to the lidar.

600-series board

A used main board with a serial port that works. This was the unlock.

Mini-DIN cable

Adafruit's 7-pin cable. It carries the Roomba's serial.

Speaker + mic

A voice. Wake word: "Stella".

Power chain

Fuse → cutoff → switch → 5 A buck. Clean 5.15 V from the Roomba battery.

3. How it fits

The main board and battery live inside the Roomba. Lidar sits on four 35 mm posts so its beam clears everything (≈ 65 mm above the plate). The two red adapters sit where the Mega used to be.

4. How it's wired

Mini-DIN colors are from my cable, traced with a meter. VPWR is raw battery (~15 V): never on the adapters or the Pi. Green (BRC) is taped off for now. No buzzer: the Roomba plays beeps itself (OI Song/Play).

The first drawing had an Arduino Mega between the Roomba and the Pi, plus an LCD and a motion sensor. The Mega died on Oct 13, so I took it out: the Roomba and the lidar now each plug into the Pi through a CP2102 adapter. The old drawing is in the blog.

7. What it took

I didn't think it would be easy, but I didn't expect how much patience it would take. The worst part is getting a part and it's the wrong one, or it's a whole different issue, and then waiting again.

I learned a lot: hosting servers on a Pi, the Arduino, every part of the robot and how it all connects to the motherboard, in a smart but messy way. I learned the hard way why the newer 694 board won't work: on the Wi-Fi model, the firmware locks you out of the serial port (the Open Interface). An older 600-series board fixed it.

After a month or two, I believe I have all the parts. I spent a lot more money than I thought I would.

Rules I learned
Get the right part the first time.
Ground first, always.
Never trust wire colors.
Never power a Pi from a power bank.

8. After the hardware worked

Once she could move and see, the rest was software.

Everything runs on the Pi. A small web server sits on top of ROS 2 and the dashboard talks to it over Tailscale, a private tunnel. There's no login: if my phone is on my tailnet I see her live, and everybody else gets the demo.

The Roomba, the lidar, the TVs and the thermostat all hang off that one server. The TVs and the Nest stay on my home network, so no cloud account is needed. Only the optional Claude brain leaves the house, and it only sees text.

9. Teaching her to listen

She listens all the time but only wakes up for her name: "Stella" or "Kalani bear." Short commands like stop, go home and TV volume work with no internet at all.

Anything else goes to Claude as text. It can only pick from a short list of safe things to do, and if the internet drops she falls back to the simple commands. She can also check on herself and tell me what's wrong: heat, power, mic, Wi-Fi, the TV, the thermostat.

The hard part was the Pi's own fan. It made her hear random commands, so now she ignores anything that isn't loud and clear, except her name.

What I learned
A fan will happily "say" stop.
Her name needs a looser bar than commands.
Let the AI pick from a list. Never let it run commands.

11. Why Stella

One Piece is one of my favorite anime. Dr. Vegapunk is one of the smartest people alive in that world, so smart he split his mind into six satellite bodies. Stella is the name of the original Vegapunk, the main body.

In the same sense, Stella has a part of me in her. We really learned and grew together.
